## 1. Background

Mitral annular calcification (MAC) predicts cardiovascular events and death [1, 2] and complicates every form of mitral intervention: it is present in 18% of surgical mitral replacements and raises operative mortality [3], and transcatheter valve-in-MAC has carried one-year mortality of 62.8% against 14.0% for valve-in-valve [4]. That balance is changing. SUMMIT-MAC, the first prospective trial of a dedicated transcatheter device for severe MAC, met its primary endpoint [5], and management of advanced disease is now a structured heart-team decision [6].

What is known about how MAC develops and advances comes from three sources. Community cohorts with serial cardiac CT, principally MESA, describe incidence and progression as a continuous calcium score in people without clinical cardiovascular disease [7]. Two echocardiographic series describe progression among patients who already have MAC: Willner and colleagues followed 11,605 patients with paired echocardiograms and found that a third of those with mild or moderate MAC reached severe MAC at ten years, more often women than men [8]; Abbasi and colleagues followed 1,420 patients with mild MAC and found that the mitral dysfunction that developed was predominantly regurgitation [9]. A CT study of 738 asymptomatic adults suggested that the factors associated with initiation differ from those associated with progression [10], and a small echocardiographic series related progression to haemodynamic load [11].

Three gaps remain. MESA examined incidence and progression in one community population by CT and concluded that atherosclerotic factors initiate MAC and are only modestly associated with its progression [7]; among the echocardiographic series identified, none followed patients from a graded MAC-free baseline through onset and onward through progression in a hospital population, and only MESA, with 280 incident cases, has modelled incidence at all. Every echocardiographic estimate treats the reported grade as accurate, although none of the studies identified has measured how reproducibly qualitative MAC grade is assigned on transthoracic echocardiography; the closest evidence is a CT study in which reader agreement was weakest for mild and moderate disease [12], and even between two core-laboratory modalities MAC scores correlated only modestly [13]. And among patients who reach disease that could be treated, the series that report it find only 17% to 35% receive mitral intervention [14–16], without examining who does not or why.

## 2. Objectives

**Primary.** Among adults under serial echocardiographic surveillance in MIMIC-IV, (a) to estimate the association of baseline characteristics with reaching moderate-or-greater MAC, separately for onset in patients graded MAC-free at index and for progression in patients graded mild, under two outcome definitions, and to test whether associations differ between the two; and (b) to characterise the reliability of the routinely reported MAC grade in this dataset: how often a first moderate-or-greater read is confirmed, refuted, or never re-examined, how often mild regresses, and the per-grade misclassification structure.

**Secondary.**
1. To estimate, by domain, the association of non-mitral echocardiographic structure, renal function, mineral metabolism, atrial fibrillation and coded comorbidity with onset and progression.
2. To estimate time to MAC-related mitral dysfunction (calcific stenosis or a raised transmitral gradient; regurgitation reported separately) after index and after moderate-or-greater MAC, and to test whether a confirmed moderate read predicts subsequent dysfunction better than a refuted one, as an internal check on the outcome definition.
3. Among patients reaching an intervenable phenotype, to describe receipt and type of mitral intervention, the characteristics associated with intervention, and survival by treatment status, as the terminal description of the trajectory.

## 3. Design

Retrospective longitudinal cohort. Linked structured echocardiography-report, hospital, laboratory, electrocardiogram machine-report and discharge-summary data from a single US academic health system, 2008 to 2022, with per-patient date shifting.

## 4. Feasibility and validation work that shaped this protocol

Before this protocol was written, the source data were examined to establish what each field means and how each planned analysis behaves. Most of it fixed definitions; some of it, in testing estimators, exclusions and baselines, produced estimates of the study's own associations under earlier specifications, and the plan was revised in their light (see Registration and disclosure). Its findings are stated here because they explain the design and will be reported descriptively in the paper.

**Grading vocabulary is stable across the study window.** The MAC field takes exactly four values (blank, mild, moderate, severe) in every calendar band from 2008 to 2022, and the moderate-or-greater share of graded studies is constant at 28% to 30%. The proportion of blank fields rises from 68% to 76% over the window, so calendar era enters every model.

**A blank field usually means the annulus was examined and not commented on.** When MAC is blank, mitral regurgitation is graded on 87% and mitral leaflets described on 92% of the same reports. Blank studies are somewhat less complete overall than graded ones (median 49 versus 53 populated fields), so a minority of blanks reflect limited studies rather than absent calcium.

**The reported grade is unstable at mild and on first appearance of moderate, in this dataset.** On consecutive episodes, a mild grade is followed by blank 44% of the time overall and 57% of the time when the mild grade had newly appeared. A moderate grade appearing for the first time above a lower grade is confirmed on the next episode in only 38% of re-examined patients from a blank baseline and 51% from a mild baseline; a moderate grade seen twice consecutively is followed by blank in 6%. Whether this reflects reader variation, image quality, or clinical state cannot be separated in report data, and it is not asserted here to be a property of echocardiography generally. It is a property of the outcome variable this study must use, and the outcome definition below is built around it.

**Same-admission repeat studies contribute noise, not information.** Seven per cent of consecutive pairs are two intensive-care studies a median of six days apart. Studies within one admission (or one calendar week outside admission) are collapsed to a single episode carrying the highest grade.

**Timing of coded exposures matters greatly.** Ascertaining comorbidities "ever" rather than "at or before the index study" inflates their prevalence by 12 to 32 percentage points and would let diagnoses made after the index echocardiogram act as predictors of it. All exposures are dated at or before index.

**Most patients have little record before their index echocardiogram.** Only 22% have a year or more of contact with the system before the index study; for 29% there is no admission before it. Coded comorbidity prevalence therefore reflects record depth as much as disease: hypertension is coded in 46% of the whole cohort but 73% of those with a year of prior record. Coded exposures are analysed in the subcohort with at least one year of prior record; measured exposures, which do not depend on record depth, are analysed in the full cohort.

**Loss to follow-up is informative.** After their last echocardiogram, 24% of patients have no further contact of any kind with the system, and these patients carry less MAC than those who die. This is stated as a limitation and addressed by inverse-probability-of-censoring weights as a sensitivity analysis.

**Confirmation depends on being re-examined, and re-examination depends on age.** Among first moderate-or-greater reads, 46% (MAC-free baseline) and 43% (mild baseline) occur on the patient's last echocardiogram; 36% to 38% of these patients die within a year, at a median of about two months. The probability of a further study after a first moderate read falls from 63% under 65 years to 42% to 43% at 85 and over, while the probability that a further study confirms the read does not vary with age. A confirmed-only outcome therefore removes noise and, at the same time, removes older patients' events, biasing the age association toward the null. The two definitions carry opposite biases (read noise in the first-observed, age-linked under-ascertainment in the confirmed), so both are primary and a concordance rule governs interpretation (Section 6).

**Modelling.** A monotone multistate model without a misclassification layer cannot be fitted to these data (roughly 12,000 consecutive pairs move to a lower grade), so any latent-state model requires a full misclassification structure; and such models absorb differences in observation frequency into their transition intensities, so their covariate effects for exposures that alter surveillance intensity (dialysis, heart failure) are not interpretable as biological. Regression on observed states with death competing is therefore the primary inferential model; the multistate model is used to characterise misclassification, not to estimate effects. The multistate model must estimate the distribution of the true state at each patient's first observation: the software default, that every patient begins truly MAC-free, is false for 30% of this cohort, and a model fitted under it attributes grade disagreement to over-calling of mild MAC. With the initial distribution estimated (a decisive likelihood improvement), the same data attribute most disagreement to under-reporting: on the full cohort, true mild is reported as blank 35% of the time and true moderate as mild 29%; a true moderate annulus is reported moderate-or-greater on 65% of studies and a true severe one on 92%; about 11% of patients whose index report is blank are estimated to be truly mild, and the reported-mild stratum is itself a mixture, about 60% true mild, 30% true MAC-free and 9% true moderate-or-greater, so "progression" in that stratum partly measures onset in mislabelled MAC-free patients and partly the re-reporting of disease already moderate; and about five in six moderate-or-greater reads are true moderate-or-greater (a model quantity, tested against outcome in Section 8.4). Estimated mean time in the true MAC-free state is about 12 years, in mild 7, in moderate 5; with a median follow-up of about three years, these are largely extrapolations under the model's constant-rate assumption. The emission matrix is reported with its confidence intervals. The severe row rests on few informative transitions: the probability that true severe is reported severe moves from 47% to 61% between the main and blank-as-censored specifications and is not quoted as a standalone reliability figure, whereas the probability that true severe is reported moderate-or-greater is 92% to 93% in both, so the unstable cell sits on the moderate-severe boundary that the outcome definition straddles rather than depends on. The model assumes reading errors independent across studies, which reader- or laboratory-level clustering would violate and which cannot be tested here because MIMIC-IV-ECHO carries no reader or sonographer identifier, and constant progression rates over time; both are stated as assumptions, not findings. Two design consequences follow. The reported-MAC-free stratum is not a verified absence of calcium, so the onset analyses are repeated in patients whose first two episodes are both blank (Section 5). And a first moderate-or-greater read that is not repeated on the next study is more often a true moderate read conservatively than a false first read, which is how the confirmed and first-observed definitions are to be interpreted against each other.

**Candidate exclusions were tested and two were rejected.** Diagnosis codes for amyloidosis and for history of irradiation were examined as exclusions: coded amyloidosis carries the cohort's own MAC prevalence and incidence, and irradiation history marks echocardiographic surveillance (these patients are echoed 37% to 63% more often per year) rather than annular disease, so neither is an exclusion or an exposure. Diagnosis codes for rheumatic mitral disease are the ICD default for unspecified mitral stenosis and, in this dataset, mostly identify calcific stenosis, so the echo-report descriptor is used instead.

**Mitral procedures after MAC are heterogeneous.** Of procedure admissions after moderate-or-greater MAC, 65% are isolated mitral procedures and 35% accompany coronary bypass or aortic valve surgery; 71% are preceded by moderate-or-greater mitral regurgitation or stenosis on the last echocardiogram. Interventions are therefore sub-classified.

## 5. Population

**Source.** Adults (age ≥18 at index) with two or more transthoracic echocardiography episodes carrying the MAC field.

**Episode.** Studies within one hospital admission, or within one calendar week outside admission, form one episode carrying the highest MAC grade recorded.

**Index.** The first episode. Patients enter the analysis stratum defined by their index grade:

| Index grade | Question |
|---|---|
| Blank (MAC-free) | onset: time to moderate-or-greater MAC |
| Mild | progression: time to moderate-or-greater MAC |
| Moderate or severe | treatment analyses only |

**Exclusions.** Mitral prosthesis or annuloplasty ring at or before index (the native annulus is no longer gradable); rheumatic mitral valve disease, defined by a "rheumatic deformity" descriptor of the mitral valve on any transthoracic report (the echo-report field, not the diagnosis code, because ICD-9 394.0 and ICD-10 I05.0 are the default codes for unspecified mitral stenosis and, in this dataset, patients carrying those codes without rheumatic morphology have four times the cohort prevalence of moderate-or-greater MAC and are predominantly calcific stenosis).

**Confirmed MAC-free baseline.** Patients whose index and second episodes are both blank, followed from the second episode. Every onset analysis is repeated in this subcohort, and an onset association is described as present only if its direction holds there as well as in the full reported-MAC-free stratum. The subcohort trades events for a cleaner baseline (about 9,300 patients with follow-up beyond the second episode; roughly 370 first-observed and 95 confirmed events; detectable hazard ratio about 1.16 and 1.33 per standard deviation).

**Follow-up.** From index to last echocardiography episode, death, or end of record.

**Subcohorts.** The full cohort for exposures measured on the index report or in laboratory data. The one-year-record subcohort (≥365 days from first system contact to index) for coded diagnoses.

## 6. Outcome definitions

**Co-primary, two definitions of the same event.** (A) First-observed: time to the first episode graded moderate or severe. (B) Confirmed: time to the first episode graded moderate or severe that is followed by a moderate or severe grade on the next episode; event time is the first of the pair. Both are interval-censored between the last lower-grade episode and the qualifying episode, and death is a competing event. Every association is reported under both. Concordance rule, fixed in advance: an association is described as present only when its direction agrees under both definitions; the magnitude is reported from both, and where the two disagree in direction the association is described as definition-dependent and not interpreted. Bridge analysis: for first moderate-or-greater reads that are the patient's last echocardiogram, confirmation status is multiply imputed (20 imputations) from a model fitted in re-examined patients on age, sex, index grade, setting, era, ejection fraction category, dialysis status and time from the read to death or end of record; the co-primary models are refitted on the imputed confirmed outcome and combined by Rubin's rules, and reported alongside the two definitions.

**What "onset" means here.** The first reported moderate-or-greater grade in a patient with no reported MAC at index. The reported MAC-free state is not a verified absence of calcium (Section 4); the estimated share of unreported mild MAC in that stratum is reported beside every onset result, and the confirmed MAC-free baseline analysis bounds its consequence.

**Mild MAC** is a reported state and is not used as an event, for the reason given in Section 4.

**Secondary: MAC-related mitral dysfunction.** Time from index, and time from first moderate-or-greater MAC, to the first episode showing mitral stenosis of moderate or greater severity or a mean transmitral gradient of 5 mmHg or more, in patients without either at index. Moderate or greater mitral regurgitation is reported as a separate endpoint and is not combined with stenosis, because in a hospital population regurgitation is dominated by functional and ischaemic mechanisms unrelated to annular calcium. Coverage of each field is stated (stenosis grade 42%, gradient 36%, regurgitation 96% of studies).

**Intervenable phenotype.** Among patients reaching moderate-or-greater MAC: first episode showing mitral regurgitation or stenosis of moderate or greater severity, or a mean transmitral gradient of 5 mmHg or more. Regurgitation grade is recorded on 96% of studies, stenosis grade on 42% and the gradient on 36%; coverage of each criterion is reported.

**Mitral intervention.** Any mitral procedure identified by ICD-9 or ICD-10-PCS procedure code, a mitral prosthesis or ring on a subsequent echocardiogram, or discharge-summary text, with concordance across routes reported. Sub-classified as isolated versus with concomitant coronary bypass or aortic valve surgery, by modality (surgical replacement, surgical repair, transcatheter edge-to-edge, transcatheter replacement), and by whether moderate-or-greater mitral dysfunction was documented on the last echocardiogram before the procedure.

## 7. Exposures

All dated at or before index. Reported in domains, so that each domain's contribution can be shown.

**Demographic.** Age; sex.

**Non-mitral echocardiographic structure, from the index report.** Aortic valve calcification grade (nl / mild / moderate / severe thickening; recorded on 86% to 91% of index reports), on the hypothesis that annular and aortic calcification share a degenerative process. It is analysed as four categories with the gradient shown, and a per-grade term only as a summary. It is a qualitative report field graded under the same conditions as the outcome; its inter-reader reproducibility has been measured only in a small study (35 studies; weighted kappa 0.52, 95% CI 0.32 to 0.72, between board-certified echocardiographers, lower for trainees) [25], and nondifferential misclassification of a polytomous exposure does not reliably bias toward the null and can distort a gradient [26, 27], so a weak or irregular gradient would be uninterpretable in direction and only a large monotone one is taken as support for the hypothesis. Simulation at that kappa (symmetric adjacent-grade error) cannot produce a monotone gradient from a null and attenuates a true one, and error that is conservative in direction, as the MAC field's is, attenuates less; a large observed gradient is therefore read as a lower bound. Left ventricular ejection fraction, categorised <40%, 40–49%, ≥50% (recorded on 78% to 84%), as the cardiac-function phenotype in place of a coded heart-failure diagnosis, with <40% versus ≥40% as a secondary cut. E/e′ (septal e′ recorded on 75% of MAC-free and mild index reports), left atrial dimension (94%) and septal wall thickness (94%) as candidate markers of haemodynamic load. Body mass index from the height and weight recorded on the echocardiography report (95% to 98%; two unit systems across eras, converted). Left ventricular ejection fraction is also an adjustment variable. All mitral and annular measurements are excluded as predictors.

Because annular calcium may itself alter tissue-Doppler velocity at the annulus [17], and because e′ is recorded less often as MAC advances (both e′ velocities present on 74% of MAC-free but 58% of moderate index reports), the load markers are tested for onset in the MAC-free stratum, where no annular calcium is present to influence the measurement, and separately for progression in the mild stratum, and the two are compared. Complete-case E/e′ estimates in the mild stratum are accompanied by multiple imputation conditional on grade.

**Renal function and mineral metabolism, from laboratory data in the 365 days before index.** (Code families for every coded exposure were checked against d_icd_diagnoses and d_icd_procedures with ICD-version restriction on 15 August 2026; the audited sets are in the analysis code.) These values are drawn predominantly during hospital admissions: creatinine is present in 77% of the cohort (99% of inpatient-index and 56% of outpatient-index patients), phosphate in 59% (91% and 30%), total calcium in 63% and alkaline phosphatase in 53%; the most recent value sits on the day of the index echocardiogram for most inpatients, and 58% of last creatinine and 69% of last phosphate values come from the index admission itself. Definitions are chosen so that a single acute-illness value does not stage chronic function. Baseline estimated glomerular filtration rate is CKD-EPI 2021 from the lowest creatinine in the window (the KDIGO baseline convention), categorised ≥60, 30–59 and <30 mL/min/1.73 m²; the median and the most recent value are sensitivity definitions, and a chronicity flag (two values below threshold at least 90 days apart) is reported but is limited by record span (39% of patients have creatinine values spanning 90 days). End-stage renal disease on maintenance dialysis by hierarchy: a chronic-dialysis or ESRD diagnosis dated at or before index, or an intermittent haemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis record at or before index; a continuous renal replacement modality counts only if preceded by a chronic marker, and otherwise defines acute kidney injury requiring renal replacement, a separate variable. Serum phosphate, total calcium and alkaline phosphatase enter as the median of the window's values, continuous per standard deviation, in the subcohort with a value, as a supporting analysis whose setting-dependent availability is stated; the most recent value and the last value drawn outside the index admission are sensitivity definitions. No hyperphosphataemia category is used: persistent hyperphosphataemia (above 4.5 mg/dL on two or more days at least a week apart) is present in 42% of dialysis-dependent patients and 4% of others, so it is a feature of a variable already in the model, and a single high value is more plausibly acute than mineral. Phosphate is associated with prevalent valvular calcification in community and CKD populations [18, 19]; in the one longitudinal community study it was not associated with MAC progression, whereas fibroblast growth factor-23 was [20]. FGF-23, parathyroid hormone (measured in 6%) and lipoprotein(a) are not available. Each analyte is tested for onset and for progression in this population, which differs from community cohorts in its burden of renal disease.

**Coded diagnoses, one-year-record subcohort.** Diabetes (ICD and Charlson-derived codings agree at 21%; type 1 is 1.5% and is not analysed separately); hypertension; coronary artery disease. Osteoporosis and hyperparathyroidism are not analysed: fewer than 30 exposed events under any definition.

**Atrial fibrillation or flutter at index, two sources.** An ICD-coded diagnosis at or before index, or atrial fibrillation or flutter read on the machine report of a 12-lead electrocardiogram within 30 days of the index echocardiogram (MIMIC-IV-ECG). An electrocardiogram within 30 days exists for about 72% of inpatient-index and 35% of outpatient-index patients, so the combined variable is more complete in inpatients and its source composition is reported. It enters the full-cohort models as a covariate, because E/e′ and left atrial dimension are rhythm-dependent measurements, and its association is reported; because MAC precedes incident atrial fibrillation in longitudinal cohorts [21, 22], it is interpreted as a correlate, not a determinant. Medication exposures are not analysed: statin and anticoagulant associations in this design are confounded by indication, and anticoagulant prevalence at index (vitamin K antagonist 7%, direct oral anticoagulant 3%) is too low to test the vitamin-K hypothesis; both are noted as questions for a cohort built for them.

**Calendar era and care setting at index** enter every model.

## 8. Analysis plan

**8.1 Descriptive.** Cohort flow with every exclusion counted. Baseline characteristics by index stratum and by subcohort. Serial-consistency results from Section 4 reported as the study's characterisation of its outcome variable.

**8.2 Primary inference.** The event is known only to lie between the last lower-grade episode and the qualifying episode, and the width of that interval is the patient's surveillance interval, so an estimator that places the event at the qualifying episode date credits frequently examined patients with earlier events. The primary estimator is therefore a piecewise-exponential proportional-hazards model on person-episode intervals (one record per inter-episode interval; hazard constant within each interval; log interval length as offset; follow-up-time bands for the baseline hazard), which is the interval-censored formulation for panel data; the cause-specific Cox model with the event at the qualifying-episode date is fitted as the companion and any divergence is reported. Death is a competing event, handled by cause-specific hazards; Fine–Gray subdistribution hazards are reported alongside for age, ejection fraction, renal function and dialysis, because deaths outnumber confirmed events by roughly seven to one in the MAC-free stratum. Missing covariates (E/e′, eGFR, left atrial dimension, wall thickness, body mass index, aortic valve grade, ejection fraction) are multiply imputed by chained equations (20 imputations) for the adjusted models, with complete-case analysis as a sensitivity and the effective sample size stated for every model, since joint completeness of the structural and renal covariates is below half the cohort. Then the multiple-imputation bridge for unverified reads (Section 6). The study is estimation-focused: it reports associations with confidence intervals, applies no family-wise correction, and pre-specifies one hypothesis test, the stratum-by-predictor interaction in Section 8.3; the concordance rule governs interpretation and is not a multiplicity control. Hazard ratios per standard deviation for continuous exposures, per category otherwise. Domain-wise models (demographic; plus echocardiographic structure; plus renal function, that is baseline eGFR category and dialysis, in the full cohort; plus mineral analytes, in the subcohort with values; plus coded diagnoses, in the one-year-record subcohort), each reported so that the incremental contribution of each domain is visible and so that a change between domains can be attributed to adjustment rather than to a change of analysis sample, which is stated whenever the sample changes. For anthropometric and comorbidity exposures (body mass index, atrial fibrillation, diabetes, hypertension, coronary disease), the association adjusted for demographic factors only ("total") is reported beside the fully adjusted association ("direct"), because these exposures are expected to act partly through the structural and renal variables in the full model; index-time measurements cannot establish mediation and the table is described as consistent with it, not as proof.

**8.3 Onset versus progression.** The same predictor set fitted in the MAC-free and mild strata; a stratum-by-predictor interaction tests whether an association differs between onset and progression. This is the least powered analysis in the protocol (at 80% power it detects a ratio of hazard ratios of about 1.12 under the first-observed definition and 1.24 under the confirmed), so the first-observed definition is primary for this test, the confirmed definition is its concordance check, and the interaction is re-examined under the confirmed MAC-free baseline, since unreported mild MAC in the reported-MAC-free stratum would narrow the contrast.

**8.4 Reliability of the reported grade.** The confirmed, refuted and never-re-examined proportions of first moderate-or-greater reads by baseline grade, age, sex, ejection fraction and dialysis status; regression of mild; and, as an internal check on the outcome definition, time to MAC-related mitral dysfunction after a confirmed versus a refuted first moderate read. **Misclassification model.** A hidden Markov multistate model with a full emission matrix and the initial-state distribution estimated, fitted to characterise per-grade misclassification in this dataset; a variant in which a blank field is treated as a censored observation known only to be MAC-free or mild is fitted as a sensitivity. Reported: the emission matrix; the probability that true mild is reported blank; the estimated share of unreported mild MAC among reported-MAC-free index reports; the model-implied positive predictive value of a moderate-or-greater read (a model quantity, not a validation; the mitral-dysfunction check above is the outcome-anchored test); and mean sojourn times. Its covariate effects are not used for inference, for the reason in Section 4.

**8.5 Informative observation and censoring.** Inverse-intensity weights from a proportional-intensity model for time to next episode as a bounding sensitivity analysis; a cruder companion in which the log of the patient's echocardiography rate enters the model directly, reported for every determinant, since a determinant that is surveillance rather than biology collapses under it. Inverse-probability-of-censoring weights for loss to follow-up as a second.

**8.6 Predictor selection.** Candidate predictors ranked by penalised Cox regression on a development half, patient-level split; the parsimonious set carried to the evaluation half. Ranking and evaluation never share patients.

**8.7 Bounded machine learning.** Gradient boosting on the same predictors at each transition as a check on linearity, reported whether or not it improves discrimination; SHAP on the transition-specific models to display predictor ranking at onset versus progression.

**8.8 Mitral dysfunction.** Cause-specific Cox regression for the secondary endpoint, from index in each stratum and from first moderate-or-greater MAC, with reaching moderate-or-greater MAC as a time-varying exposure in the from-index models.

**8.9 Intervention, terminal description.** Among patients with an intervenable phenotype: logistic regression for receipt of any mitral intervention on age at the phenotype episode, sex, ejection fraction category, eGFR category, dialysis, phenotype type (regurgitant or stenotic) and atrial fibrillation, following the analytical template of the Euro Heart Survey analysis of denied mitral surgery [23]; repeated with isolated intervention as the outcome. Survival from the phenotype episode with intervention as a time-varying exposure to avoid immortal-time bias, reported as an association under confounding by indication.

**8.10 Uncertainty.** Patient-level bootstrap or repeated split resampling for all intervals; models compared within resample.

## 9. Sample size

Fixed by the data. Feasibility counts, which fix power but are not results: 31,532 adults with two or more episodes; 388 removed for a prosthesis or ring at or before index and 204 for rheumatic morphology; 30,940 analysed, of whom 21,676 are MAC-free at index, 6,944 mild and 2,320 moderate or severe. Under the first-observed definition, 1,036 events in the MAC-free stratum and 1,600 in the mild stratum; under the confirmed definition, 249 and 527. Deaths before any progression exceed confirmed events in both strata (1,872 and 705). Of first-observed moderate-or-greater reads, 46% (MAC-free) and 43% (mild) occur on the patient's last echocardiogram; 36% to 38% of those patients die within a year, at a median of about two months. Median follow-up to the last echocardiogram is 3.2 years in the MAC-free stratum, 2.9 in the mild stratum and 1.8 in the moderate-or-severe stratum. At 80% power and two-sided alpha 0.05 the confirmed definition detects a hazard ratio of about 1.19 per standard deviation in the MAC-free stratum and 1.13 in the mild stratum; the first-observed definition about 1.09 and 1.07. The coded-exposure subcohort carries 235 and 353 first-observed events (55 and 102 confirmed) and is a supporting analysis for that reason. Acute kidney injury requiring renal replacement at or before index is present in 0.1% to 0.2% and is described but not modelled.

## 10. Bias, and how each is addressed

Left truncation: index is the first echocardiogram in this system, not in life; stratification by index grade, and the one-year-record subcohort as a partial check. Record depth: measured exposures in the full cohort, coded exposures in the subcohort, and the difference stated. Outcome misclassification and verification: two co-primary definitions with opposite biases, a concordance rule, a multiple-imputation bridge for unverified reads, and the hidden Markov characterisation [24]. Informative observation and censoring: Section 8.5, with the limits of weighting stated. Immortal time: time-varying intervention. Confounding by indication: labelled, no causal language for medication. Reverse causation within exposures: load markers tested by stratum; atrial fibrillation treated as a correlate. Competing death: handled throughout and reported prominently.

## 11. Limitations acknowledged in advance

The reported grade is a category assigned in routine practice, not a quantitative score, and its instability in this dataset is characterised rather than corrected. Findings about grade consistency describe MIMIC-IV-ECHO and are not asserted to generalise to other laboratories. The population is a referred hospital population; associations may transport, absolute rates will not. Prior echocardiograms outside the system are unseen. Coded exposures reflect record depth as well as disease. Analytes that prior work identified as informative (FGF-23, PTH, lipoprotein(a)) are unavailable. The confirmed outcome requires a further echocardiogram, and the probability of a further echocardiogram after a first moderate read falls with age (63% under 65 years, 42% to 43% at 85 and over) while the probability of confirmation given a further study does not; the confirmed definition therefore under-ascertains events differentially in older patients and biases the age association toward the null, which is why the two definitions are reported side by side and age is interpreted across both. Sex differences in progression, if found, replicate rather than extend Willner and colleagues.
